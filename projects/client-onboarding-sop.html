Project 03

Client Onboarding SOP & Checklist

A documented onboarding SOP and operational checklist with explicit approval controls, so every new client starts the same way.

This is a self-directed, fictional scenario-based demonstration. It is not commissioned client work, and the impact described below is expected, not measured.

The scenarioA growing firm with an informal onboarding routine

Imagine a small professional-services firm that onboards new clients. The steps live in people's heads, so each onboarding looks a little different depending on who handles it.

1 · ProblemInconsistent steps lead to gaps

Without a documented process, onboarding can end with missing documents, unclear responsibilities and incomplete records.

  • Required documents are requested at different times, or not at all
  • It is unclear who owns each step
  • There is no explicit point where someone confirms the file is complete

2 · DiagnosisWhat I would look for first

  • The steps as they are actually done today, from first contact to kick-off
  • Which documents and details are required for every client
  • Who is responsible at each stage, and who must approve
  • Where files and records are stored

3 · SolutionA written procedure with clear sign-off points

  • SOP document: purpose, scope, roles, step-by-step procedure and record-keeping rules
  • Operational checklist: a one-page list used for every new client, with a status for each step
  • Approval controls: named approval points, such as confirming documents are complete before the client is marked ready, recorded with approver and date

4 · ProcessHow I would build and test it

  1. Understand: confirm what a complete onboarding looks like for the firm
  2. Diagnose: compare the current steps against that standard
  3. Design: write the SOP and build the checklist with approval points
  4. Test & refine: walk a sample client through it and revise unclear steps

5 · Expected impactWhat this is designed to support

These are intended benefits of the design. No measured results are claimed.

  • A consistent onboarding experience regardless of who handles it
  • Fewer missing documents, because required items are listed and checked
  • Easier handover, because the process is written down
