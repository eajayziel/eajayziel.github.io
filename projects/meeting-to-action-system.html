Project 02

Meeting-to-Action System

Meeting documentation, an action tracker, deadline monitoring and an executive overview that turn decisions into follow-through.

This is a self-directed, fictional scenario-based demonstration. It is not commissioned client work, and the impact described below is expected, not measured.

The scenarioA team that meets often and follows up unevenly

Imagine a small consulting team that holds weekly planning meetings and client calls. Good decisions are made in the room, but afterwards it is not always clear who is doing what, or by when.

1 · ProblemDecisions lose momentum without ownership

When actions, owners and deadlines are not recorded and tracked consistently, decisions stall and the same topics return at the next meeting.

  • Notes record discussion but not clear actions
  • Actions have no named owner or due date
  • No one reviews open actions between meetings

2 · DiagnosisWhat I would look for first

  • How meetings are currently recorded and where notes are stored
  • Which decisions typically need follow-up, and from whom
  • How and when owners are reminded
  • What the executive needs to see about open actions

3 · SolutionNotes that feed a live action tracker

  • Meeting notes template: attendees, decisions, actions and open questions in a consistent format
  • Action tracker: every action with owner, due date, status and source meeting
  • Deadline monitoring: highlights for items that are due soon or overdue
  • Executive overview: a one-page summary of open, due-soon and completed actions

4 · ProcessHow I would build and test it

  1. Understand: learn how the team meets and what decisions need tracking
  2. Diagnose: review sample notes for missing owners and dates
  3. Design: create the notes template, tracker and overview
  4. Test & refine: run a sample meeting through the system and fix anything unclear

5 · Expected impactWhat this is designed to support

These are intended benefits of the design. No measured results are claimed.

  • Clearer ownership of every action agreed in a meeting
  • Earlier visibility of overdue or at-risk items
  • Less time spent re-discussing decisions that were already made
