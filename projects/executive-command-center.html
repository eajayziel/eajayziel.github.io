Project 01

Executive Weekly Command Center

A structured executive dashboard and task-tracking workbook that brings tasks, deadlines and priorities into one view.

This is a self-directed, fictional scenario-based demonstration. It is not commissioned client work, and the impact described below is expected, not measured.

The scenarioA busy executive, a crowded week

Imagine a founder who runs client work, a small team and their own business development. Tasks arrive by email, chat, meetings and notes to self. Deadlines live in a calendar, in a notebook and in memory.

1 · ProblemTasks, deadlines and priorities are hard to oversee

When information is spread across several places, it is difficult to see what is due, what matters most this week and what is waiting on someone else.

  • No single view of the week ahead
  • Priorities compete with urgent but smaller requests
  • Items waiting on other people are easy to lose track of

2 · DiagnosisWhat I would look for first

  • Where tasks currently come from and where they are recorded
  • Which deadlines are fixed and which can move
  • What the executive needs to see at a glance, and how often
  • Where follow-ups tend to be missed

3 · SolutionOne workbook, one weekly rhythm

A structured Excel workbook with a clear separation between data entry and what the executive reads.

  • Dashboard: this week's priorities, due-soon items and overdue items in one view
  • Task tracker: one row per task with owner, priority, due date, status and notes
  • Waiting-on list: items that depend on other people, with follow-up dates
  • Weekly review: a short checklist for closing out the week and setting the next

4 · ProcessHow I would build and test it

  1. Understand: confirm the executive's priorities, reporting needs and tools
  2. Diagnose: map where tasks and deadlines currently sit
  3. Design: build the tracker, dashboard views and weekly review routine
  4. Test & refine: check formulas and filters with sample data, then adjust to how it is actually used

5 · Expected impactWhat this is designed to support

These are intended benefits of the design. No measured results are claimed.

  • Faster weekly planning, because priorities and deadlines are in one place
  • Fewer missed follow-ups, because waiting-on items have dates
  • A repeatable weekly review that can be handed to another assistant

DeliverablesWhat is included

Excel workbookDashboard, task tracker, waiting-on list and weekly review
